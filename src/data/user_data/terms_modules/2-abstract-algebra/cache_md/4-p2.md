#### Committee selection (with roles).

Committee selection (with roles).
:p From 12 people, how many ways can we form a 4person committee and, from among those four, designate a chair and a distinct secretary?
??x
Solution. General formulas used.
- Combinations: $\binom{n}{k}=\frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!}$.
- Partial permutations for labeling $r$ distinct roles among $k$ people: ${ }_k P_r=k(k- 1) \cdots(k-r+1)=\frac{k!}{(k-r)!}$.

Work. Choose the 4 members: $\binom{12}{4}$. Assign two distinct roles (chair, secretary) among the $4:{ }_4 P_2=4 \cdot 3$. Hence

$$
\binom{12}{4} \cdot{ }_4 P_2=\binom{12}{4} \cdot 12=\frac{12 \cdot 11 \cdot 10 \cdot 9}{4 \cdot 3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1} \cdot 12=495 \cdot 12=5940
$$

#### Committee selection (forbidden pairs).
 Committee selection (forbidden pairs). From 12 people we must choose a 5 -person committee. Two pairs of people, $\{A, B\}$ and $\{C, D\}$, refuse to serve together (i.e., a valid committee may not contain both $A$ and $B$, and may not contain both $C$ and $D$ ). 

:p How many valid committees are there?
??x

Solution. General formula used (Inclusion-Exclusion Principle). For sets $U$ and "bad" properties $E_1, E_2$,

$$
\left|U \backslash\left(E_1 \cup E_2\right)\right|=|U|-\left|E_1\right|-\left|E_2\right|+\left|E_1 \cap E_2\right| .
$$


Work. Total committees: $\binom{12}{5}$. Let $E_1$ be committees containing both $A$ and $B$. Then choose 3 more from the other 10: $\left|E_1\right|=\binom{10}{3}$. Similarly $\left|E_2\right|=\binom{10}{3}$ for containing both $C$ and $D$. For $E_1 \cap E_2$, the committee contains $A, B, C, D$ and 1 more from the remaining 8: $\binom{8}{1}$. Therefore

$$
\binom{12}{5}-\binom{10}{3}-\binom{10}{3}+\binom{8}{1}=\binom{12}{5}-2\binom{10}{3}+8=792-2 \cdot 120+8=560
$$

#### Pigeonhole Principle (complementary sums)
Pigeonhole principle (complementary sums). Show that among any 13 distinct integers chosen from $\{1,2, \ldots, 24\}$ there must be two that sum to 25 .

??x
Solution. General statement (Pigeonhole Principle). If $N$ objects are placed into $m$ boxes and $N>m$, then some box contains at least two objects. Work. Partition $\{1, \ldots, 24\}$ into 12 complementary pairs that sum to 25 :
$$
(1,24),(2,23), \ldots,(12,13) .
$$
These 12 pairs are the "boxes". Choosing 13 numbers (the "objects") forces two numbers to fall in the same pair, hence they sum to 25 . Therefore the claim holds.

#### Circular permutations (avoiding adjacency).
Eight distinct people are to sit around a round table. In how many ways can they be seated if Alice and Bob are not allowed to sit next to each other? (Rotations are considered the same; reflections are not identified.)
??x
Solution. General formulas used.
- Circular permutations of $n$ distinct objects (up to rotation): $(n-1)$ !.
- To count "together" adjacency of two labeled people in a circle: treat them as a block (with 2 internal orders), giving $(n-1-1)!\cdot 2=(n-2)!\cdot 2$ arrangements.
- Complement counting: \#(not adjacent) $=(n-1)!-2(n-2)$ !.

Work. Total circular seatings: $(8-1)!=7!=5040$. Count seatings where Alice and Bob are adjacent: treat $\{\mathrm{A}, \mathrm{B}\}$ as a block; we have 7 entities (the block plus 6 others), yielding $(7-1)!=6!$ circular arrangements, and 2 internal orders for $\mathrm{A} / \mathrm{B}$. So adjacent count is $2 \cdot 6!=2 \cdot 720=1440$. Therefore

$$
7!-2 \cdot 6!=5040-1440=3600
$$

Generating functions / coin-change (small example). How many ways are there to make change for 10 cents using unlimited coins of denominations 1,2 , and 5 (order of coins does not matter)?
Solution (elementary counting). Let $k$ be the number of 5 -cent coins: $k=0,1,2$.
- If $k=0$, we must solve $a+2 b=10$ with $a, b \geq 0$. For each $b=0,1,2,3,4,5$ there is a unique $a$, so 6 solutions.
- If $k=1$, remaining $a+2 b=5$. Values $b=0,1,2$ give 3 solutions.
- If $k=2$, remaining $a+2 b=0$ gives 1 solution.

Total ways: $6+3+1=10$. So 10 solutions.
Remark (generating function). The generating function is

$$
\frac{1}{(1-x)\left(1-x^2\right)\left(1-x^5\right)}
$$

and the coefficient of $x^{10}$ equals 10.
