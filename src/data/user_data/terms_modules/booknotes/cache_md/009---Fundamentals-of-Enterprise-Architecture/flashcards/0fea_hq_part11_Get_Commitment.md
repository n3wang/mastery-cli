#### Framing Concerns as Part of Trade-Off Analysis
When stakeholders express concerns, it's important to integrate those concerns into the decision-making process as part of a trade-off analysis. This ensures that the decision is transparent and that all implications are understood. If a concern cannot be resolved by changing the decision, it should be accepted as a risk and documented.

:p How should stakeholder concerns be handled when they cannot be resolved through changes to the decision?
??x
Stakeholder concerns should be:
1. Integrated into the architecture decision’s trade-off analysis.
2. Documented as part of the decision record, especially if they must be accepted as risks.
3. Validated by stakeholders through review and concurrence.
4. Presented in a tangible artifact such as a document or meeting minutes.

This process ensures that the decision reflects a balanced understanding of all implications and that there is no assumption of alignment without explicit confirmation.

#### Siloed Teams and Cross-Team Collaboration
In large organizations, teams may operate in silos, each focusing on their own domain without coordination. This can lead to conflicting solutions or duplicated efforts, especially when teams work on related but distinct problems such as data management and data privacy.

:p Why do siloed teams lead to inefficiencies and conflicts?
??x
Siloed teams often develop solutions independently, without considering the broader context or impact on other teams. This leads to duplicated efforts, inconsistent data handling, and potential conflicts in architecture or compliance. Coordination and cross-team alignment are essential to avoid such issues.

$$
\text{Conflict} = \frac{\text{Overlapping Responsibilities}}{\text{Communication} \times \text{Alignment}}
$$

If communication is low, even small overlaps can lead to significant conflict.

#### Knowledge Management in Enterprise Architecture
Knowledge management refers to the processes and tools used to create, share, and apply knowledge within an organization. In enterprise architecture, this is especially important because architecture decisions must be made consistently and effectively across teams and projects.
:p How does knowledge management support enterprise architecture?
??x
Knowledge management supports enterprise architecture by teaching users how to apply architecture knowledge to solve similar or new problems. For example, understanding patterns like event-driven architecture and ETL processes allows teams to combine these concepts when building systems to process real-time data. It ensures that learning and best practices are shared across the organization.
