#### Small-Step Project Approach
Background context: The text suggests starting with a small, manageable project to test the waters and build trust with clients before embarking on larger commitments. This approach can help alleviate concerns about the process and ensure smoother negotiations.

:p What is the initial step suggested for managing pressured contracts?

??x
The initial step is to contract with the client for a small-step project where you work together on a small piece of the total project so that the client can evaluate whether the process meets their expectations. This allows them to experience your work firsthand, potentially lowering concerns.

For example:
- You might start by working on a specific feature or module.
- Provide clear milestones and checkpoints for evaluation.

#### Continuous Negotiation
The text emphasizes that negotiation is an ongoing process throughout the life of the project, not just during contracting. This continuous nature can lead to long delays and uncertainties.

:p Explain why constant negotiation is necessary even after the initial contract phase.
??x
Negotiations are needed because the project requirements and circumstances evolve over time. The initial agreement might need adjustments as new information comes in or as the project progresses. Continuous dialogue helps ensure that both parties remain aligned with each other's expectations, which can prevent misunderstandings and disputes later on.

For example, a project might start with an initial scope but require modifications based on changing business needs or unforeseen challenges. Regular negotiation keeps everyone informed and ensures that any changes are mutually agreed upon.

#### Problem Definition Overkill
The issue highlighted here is consultants often spend too much time defining the problem during initial meetings, which can consume valuable negotiation time.

:p What should be done to avoid spending too much time on problem definition?
??x
You should limit the discussion of the presenting problem to no more than 35% of your meeting time. Instead, focus on negotiating wants and addressing concerns related to control and exposure.

If you find yourself struggling with defining the problem, try breaking the ice by directly asking the client what they want from you or making a statement that guides the conversation towards actionable steps. This helps shift the focus away from over-defining the issue and towards finding solutions.

The context involves situations where there is an intermediary between the consultant and the manager, which can create barriers to direct communication.

:p How should you handle a go-between who might be too active or protective?
