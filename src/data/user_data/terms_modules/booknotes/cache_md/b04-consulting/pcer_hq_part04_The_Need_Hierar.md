#### Business Needs
Business needs are the highest-order need and all other needs emanate from them. In a for-profit organization, they typically aim to achieve goals like growing market share, increasing operational efficiency, or decreasing costs. For non-profit organizations, these might include raising enrollment, improving member satisfaction, or contributing more to reserves. Governmental organizations may focus on citizen satisfaction with services provided.

These needs are usually quantifiable and measurable, such as revenue growth, customer satisfaction scores, or budget adherence.

Business needs represent the overarching goals that an organization aims to achieve. They drive all other levels of need, including performance, organizational capability, and individual capabilities. These are often quantifiable and measurable objectives like market share growth or operational cost reduction.

#### Performance Needs
Performance needs focus on what individuals must do more, better, or differently in order to achieve the business goals. They describe on-the-job accomplishments and behaviors required of employees within their specific roles. Star performers are typically used as a benchmark to identify these performance needs.

For example, interviewing star project managers reveals that they consistently follow a documented process and obtain specific requirements from users.
:p What is the focus of performance needs in performance consulting?
??x
Performance needs focus on the behaviors and accomplishments required by employees to meet business goals. They are identified through analyzing the actions of top performers and comparing them with those of average performers.

For instance, if star project managers follow a documented process consistently while typical project managers do not, this gap identifies areas for improvement.

#### Organizational Capability Needs
Organizational capability needs refer to the infrastructure that supports and enables practices required to achieve business goals. This includes both tangible (like vehicles, computers) and intangible factors (norms, incentives).

For example, in a situation where project managers are struggling with on-time delivery and budget adherence, organizational factors like the presence of necessary technology, equipment, or a proven team formation process should be investigated.
:p What do organizational capability needs cover?
??x
Organizational capability needs encompass the infrastructure that supports and enables practices required to achieve business goals. This includes both tangible elements such as physical resources (vehicles, computers) and intangible factors like norms, incentives, clarity of expectations, and amount of authority.

For example, if project managers are struggling with on-time delivery, the presence or absence of necessary technology, equipment, and a proven team formation process would be key organizational capability needs to investigate.

#### Individual Capability Needs
Individual capability needs refer to the skills, knowledge, and attributes employees must possess to perform as required. Skills and knowledge can often be developed, but attributes like resilience are best assessed during hiring.

For example, if project teams form based on availability rather than technical competence, some team members may not meet the necessary parameters for successful project completion.
:p What do individual capability needs cover?
??x
Individual capability needs refer to the skills, knowledge, and attributes that employees must have to perform their jobs effectively. Skills and knowledge can typically be developed through training or learning interventions, whereas attributes like resilience are best assessed during hiring as they may be difficult to develop later.

For example, if project teams form based on availability rather than technical competence, this could lead to inadequate performance due to a lack of necessary skills.

#### Should-IS-Cause Logic Overview
The Should-IS-Cause logic is a tool used to analyze business and performance situations. It involves understanding what should be happening (goals) compared to what is currently happening (current state), along with identifying factors that may help or hinder achieving these goals.

This concept is crucial for strategic initiatives, where defining and aligning needs in the hierarchy requires gathering relevant information. The tool helps ensure that the business and performance goals expressed by the client are identified precisely; it organizes known and unknown information about the situation and determines what additional data must be collected to find viable solutions.
:p What does Should-IS-Cause logic primarily focus on?
??x
It focuses on understanding the difference between what should be happening in a business context (goals) and what is currently happening (current state), along with identifying factors that could help or hinder achieving these goals. This involves mapping out these gaps to drive strategic alignment.
x??

#### Gaps Map Tool Description
The Gaps Map tool was developed to guide performance consultants through the process of obtaining necessary information for addressing specific business problems or opportunities. It is structured like a map, with you (the consultant) as the navigator and your client as the driver towards a defined destination (business goal).

The tool helps in identifying gaps between what SHOULD be occurring and what IS occurring by focusing on SHOULDs (goals), ISs (current states), and CAUSEs (factors affecting goals). It ensures that the business needs are clearly articulated, relevant information is organized, and unknown but necessary data is identified to support finding practical solutions.
:p How does the Gaps Map tool assist in addressing business problems or opportunities?
??x
The Gaps Map tool assists by guiding consultants through identifying gaps between desired outcomes (SHOULDs) and current realities (ISs), while also considering causal factors (CAUSEs). It helps articulate business needs, organize relevant information, and pinpoint unknown yet necessary data to develop effective solutions. This ensures a structured approach rather than jumping directly to potential solutions.
x??

#### Business Need Identification
A business need is an operational or strategic goal that can be measured quantitatively. The Gaps Map tool aids in identifying these goals by asking consultants to determine the destination for their client's journey, much like setting a GPS destination.

In the context of business needs, this involves understanding what the client wants to achieve and how it will be measured. For example, if a client aims to increase customer satisfaction scores from 70% to 85%, that would be a quantifiable goal.

A business need in the context of performance consulting is an operational or strategic goal that can be measured quantitatively. For example, increasing customer satisfaction scores from 70% to 85% is a specific and measurable business need. This goal helps guide the identification of necessary actions and strategies.
