#### Identifying Clients and Stakeholders

Background context: When working on performance consulting initiatives, it is crucial to identify the correct individuals with whom you should partner. These are often referred to as clients. However, other roles within the organization can also play significant parts, particularly first-line supervisors or employees whose performance will change. Understanding these roles helps in tailoring solutions that align with organizational goals and garner necessary support.

The key distinction between a client and a stakeholder is that **clients** own and are accountable for achieving business results addressed by the initiative, whereas stakeholders have a vested interest but not full accountability.

For example, if an organization aims to improve customer service ratings:
- The **client** might be the Vice President of Customer Service or the Chief Operating Officer.
- **Stakeholders** could include first-line supervisors, since they manage teams directly interacting with customers and thus can influence outcomes significantly.

:p Who are clients in performance consulting?
??x
Clients are individuals who own (have accountability for) achieving business results within the organization. They have authority to make things happen, including garnering resources needed for a specific initiative. They are typically found in mid-to-upper management levels such as president, CEO, vice president, etc.

:p Who are stakeholders in performance consulting?
??x
Stakeholders have a vested interest in the outcome of an initiative and may be accountable for achieving some portion of the results within their span of control but do not own or fully account for all business results. For example, first-line supervisors whose teams directly interact with customers.

:p Why is direct access to clients important?
??x
Direct access to clients is crucial because performance consultants need to influence decision-making related to performance and business goals. Without direct access, it's nearly impossible to effectively influence someone’s decisions.

#### Sustained vs Project Clients

Background context: In performance consulting, distinguishing between sustained and project clients helps in managing relationships more effectively. Sustained clients maintain a continuous partnership independent of specific projects due to their critical role in organizational goals. Project clients are involved for the duration of particular initiatives but not beyond.

:p What is the difference between sustained and project clients?
??x
Sustained clients are individuals with whom you have a consistent, ongoing relationship independent of any current project or initiative because they hold significant influence over business results (e.g., CEO, VP). Project clients are involved for specific projects only; their involvement decreases once the project is completed. They may be lower in the organizational hierarchy.

#### Identifying Strategic Opportunities

Performance consultants identify opportunities to improve business results, which can be reactive or proactive. Reactive identification happens when a client requests a specific solution, whereas proactive discussions aim to raise awareness of future goals and potential performance issues.

Reframing techniques involve focusing on the desired results rather than immediately jumping to solutions.
:p What is the difference between reactive and proactive approaches in identifying strategic opportunities?
??x
In the reactive approach, clients request a specific solution, and the consultant's task is to reframe this request to focus on the outcomes they seek. In contrast, the proactive method involves initiating discussions with the client about future goals and discussing performance issues that are not currently on their radar but could impact business results.
x??

#### Strategic vs. Tactical Situations

Decisions must be made whether a need should be addressed in a strategic or tactical manner. A strategic approach requires some level of assessment, while a tactical one moves directly into solution implementation.

Strategic opportunities meet specific criteria: there is direct support for one or more business needs, the consultant has access to accountable clients, performance change involves multiple employee groups, accountability for results is shared with the client, and time and access to necessary people are provided.
:p What criteria must be met for a situation to qualify as a strategic opportunity?
??x
For a situation to be considered strategic, it must meet these criteria:
1. One or more business needs are directly supported by the initiative.
2. The consultant has direct access to clients accountable for achieving those business needs.
3. The client seeks performance change from people in one or more employee groups.
4. The client is willing to share accountability with the consultant for producing the required change.
5. The client provides time and access to appropriate people so that necessary information can be gathered before deciding on and implementing solutions.
6. The situation focuses on a group of people, not on a few individuals.

These criteria ensure that strategic initiatives are well-supported and have clear goals and accountability mechanisms in place.

#### Reframing Techniques

Reframing involves reinterpreting the client's request to focus on desired results rather than immediately jumping to solutions. This technique helps in aligning the solution with broader business objectives.

For example, if a manager requests more training for a team, reframing might involve discussing how improved performance or specific outcomes can be achieved through training.
:p What does reframe mean in the context of performance consulting?
??x
Reframing means reinterpreting the client's request to focus on desired results rather than immediately jumping to solutions. This technique helps align the proposed solution with broader business objectives and ensures that the initiative is targeted at achieving specific, measurable outcomes.

For instance, if a line manager requests more training for their team, reframing this might involve discussing how improved performance or specific outcomes can be achieved through enhanced training.
