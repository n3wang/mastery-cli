#### Indicators of Scope Seep
There are several conditions that can lead consultants into the trap of scope seep: perfection-over-success, tidy-up detour, mine-is-bigger-than-yours pitfall, oops belly flop, and ego catch-wire. These can be as addictive as drugs, so it's crucial to maintain discipline.
:p What are some common indicators of scope seep?
??x
Common indicators of scope seep include:
1. Perfection-over-success: Intervening in areas where better or faster solutions exist.
2. Tidy-up detour: Fixing minor issues like meeting agendas or peer conflicts out of habit.
3. Mine-is-bigger-than-yours pitfall: Spending time trying to use unused methodologies on the project, even if not paid for.
4. Oops belly flop: Addressing major errors in assumptions without involving the client as a partner.
5. Ego catch-wire: Sharing knowledge with others who are not actively engaged, risking loss of focus.

#### Midcourse Corrections and Adaptive Actions
Midcourse corrections are necessary adjustments made during engagements due to various unexpected factors.

Background context: These corrections help in managing the complexities of real-world projects where initial assumptions may not hold true. The author emphasizes that organizations often face multiple challenges simultaneously, requiring flexible approaches.
:p What are some typical causes for midcourse corrections?
??x
Some typical causes for midcourse corrections include:
- Buyer's bad assumptions verified as incorrect
- Inadequate support from the buyer
- Key personnel unavailability due to vacations or emergencies
- Competitive moves by rivals
- Emergence of new technologies or their failures
- Customer desertion
- Unexpected rush of new business
- Resistance within the organization

These factors can significantly impact project timelines and outcomes, necessitating adaptive actions.
