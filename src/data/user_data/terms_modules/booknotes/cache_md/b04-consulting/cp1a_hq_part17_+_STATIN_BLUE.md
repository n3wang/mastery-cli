#### Determining Cost Growth Proportionality
Background context: The COO of Smackdown Rivals is concerned about whether costs are rising out of proportion to revenue growth. The company needs to ensure its cost structure supports aggressive international expansion and strategic investments while maintaining profitability. To address this, we need to calculate the year-over-year (YOY) growth rates for both revenue and costs.
:p Calculate the YOY growth rate for costs and compare it with the YOY growth rate of revenue.
??x
To determine if costs are rising out of proportion to revenue growth, follow these steps:

1. **Identify Revenue and Cost Data**: Use the provided financial statements or charts to find the revenue and cost data for each year.

2. **Calculate Growth Rates**:
   - $$ \text{YOY\% Growth} = \left( \frac{\text{Current Year Value} - \text{Previous Year Value}}{\text{Previous Year Value}} \right) \times 100 $$
   
3. **Compare Growth Rates**: Compare the calculated YOY growth rates of revenue and costs.

For example, if the revenue in 2007 was $500 million and in 2008 it was $526.5 million:
$$ \text{YOY\% Revenue} = \left( \frac{526.5 - 500}{500} \right) \times 100 = 5.3\% $$

If the SG&A costs in 2007 were $30 million and in 2008 they were $45 million:
$$ \text{YOY\% Costs} = \left( \frac{45 - 30}{30} \right) \times 100 = 50\% $$

If the YOY growth rate of costs (50%) is significantly higher than that of revenue (5.3%), it suggests that costs are rising out of proportion to revenue.

You might also consider breaking down costs by categories such as SG&A, bad debt, advertising and promotion, etc., using additional charts provided.
