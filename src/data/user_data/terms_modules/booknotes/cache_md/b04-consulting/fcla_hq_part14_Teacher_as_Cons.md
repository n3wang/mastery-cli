#### Specific Dimensions of Discovery
Here are some specific dimensions to explore:
- Objectives: Determine goal clarity and agreement.
- Subgroups: Relationships, support, conflict, exclusion.
- Support: How it is expressed (silence can be a form of support).
- Evaluation: Norms for feedback.
- Positive history: Stories of what made the organization great.
- Status differences: High and low status groups and their impacts.
- Authority and power: Dynamics between high and low-power individuals.
- Decision-making: Role of the boss, viewpoints in decisions.
- Norms for behavior: Taking initiative, disagreement, risks.
- Management information: Resource identification, problem identification.
- Leadership style: Formal and informal leaders and their impact.
- Conflict management: Methods used to handle conflict.
- Domination: Impact of dominant individuals or groups.
- Project attitudes: Attitudes towards the project and your involvement.
- Diversity: Handling differences in culture, race, and positions.
- Going forward: Recommendations and hopes for progress.
:p What are some specific dimensions consultants should explore during discovery?
??x
Consultants should explore several specific dimensions such as:
1. **Objectives**: Assessing goal clarity and agreement.
2. **Subgroups**: Examining relationships among groups working on the problem, identifying supportive and conflicting groups.
3. **Support**: Understanding how support is expressed (e.g., through silence) and who receives it from whom.
4. **Evaluation**: Norms for feedback and how people find out where they stand.
5. **Positive history**: Exploring what made the organization great and its strengths.
6. **Status differences**: Identifying high and low status groups and their impacts on problem-solving and attitudes towards solutions.
7. **Authority and power**: Analyzing power dynamics, including open or cautious handling of authority differences.
8. **Decision-making**: Understanding how decisions are made within the group, role of the boss, and how viewpoints are considered.
9. **Norms for behavior**: Examining norms around taking initiative, making demands, expressing disagreement, and dealing with risks.
10. **Management information**: Identifying resources, monitoring progress, evaluating movements, and identifying problems.
11. **Leadership style**: Analyzing the styles of formal and informal leaders and their impact.
12. **Conflict management**: How conflict is handled (managed, confronted, smoothed over).
13. **Domination**: Assessing whether the situation is dominated by one or more persons and understanding their impact.
14. **Project attitudes**: Exploring how people feel about your involvement in helping with the project.
15. **Diversity**: Handling differences in culture, race, positions, and welcoming strangers.
16. **Going forward**: Gathering recommendations for making progress on the problem.
