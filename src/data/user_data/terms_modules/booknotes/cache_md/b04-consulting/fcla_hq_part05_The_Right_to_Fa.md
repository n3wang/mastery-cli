#### Client Control on Sharing Findings
Consultants have the luxury of giving clients control over who sees the findings. This approach is particularly beneficial when acting as an outside consultant, but inside consultants might not have this flexibility.

When drafting a confidentiality clause, it's important to specify:
- The primary recipient: For example, "The results will be given to the director of engineering."
- Secondary recipients and client involvement: "Any further reporting will be up to the director. Should internal consultants be required to report any results to the larger organization, the director will be informed and invited to attend any meetings held on the subject."

For a difficult case:
- Initial reporting: "The results of the audit will be reported to the management audit committee."
- Client review: "Before the report is released, the division controller will be able to review and comment on the audit findings and recommendations."
- Final approval: "The intent (and common practice) is that the audit report goes to the committee with the support of both the division controller and the audit team. The final report also includes the list of corrective actions that the division plans to take."

:p How should an outside consultant handle client control over sharing findings?
??x
An outside consultant can provide a clause in the contract stating, "The results of this study will be given to the director of engineering [the client]. Any further reporting will be up to the director. Should internal consultants be required to report any results to the larger organization, the director will be informed and invited to attend any meetings held on the subject." This ensures that clients have control over who sees the findings.
