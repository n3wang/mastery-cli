#### Percentage Calculations in Case Interviews
Background context: Understanding how to calculate percentages is essential for various scenarios like market share, cost analysis, etc. It's important to be able to work with percentages quickly and accurately.

:p What are some common percentage calculation problems in case interviews?
??x
Common percentage calculation problems include finding market share, calculating costs per unit, determining labor costs, and more. For example:

- **Market Share**: If the total widget market is $170 million and your sales are $30 million:
  $$\text{Percentage Market Share} = \left(\frac{30}{170}\right) \times 100$$

- **Cost Per Unit**: If manufacturing costs are $20 million for 39,379 units:
  $$\text{Cost per Unit} = \frac{20,000,000}{39,379}$$

#### Calculating Percentage Change in Case Interviews
Background context: Calculating percentage change is useful for scenarios involving price changes or growth rates.

C/Java code or pseudocode:
```java
// Pseudocode for calculating percentage change
public double calculatePercentageChange(double originalValue, double newValue) {
    return ((newValue - originalValue) / originalValue) * 100;
}
```

:p How do you calculate the percentage change between two values?
??x
To calculate the percentage change:
$$\text{Percentage Change} = \left(\frac{\text{New Value} - \text{Original Value}}{\text{Original Value}}\right) \times 100$$

For example, if a stock price changes from $27 to $31:
$$\text{Percentage Increase} = \left(\frac{31 - 27}{27}\right) \times 100$$

#### Summarizing Cases

**Background Context:**
Summaries should be concise and highlight key points or recommendations. If the case involves comparing multiple strategies or options, creating a final slide can significantly enhance your performance. This helps keep all relevant information organized and makes it easier for the interviewer to follow.

:p How do you create effective summaries?
??x
To create effective summaries, provide a brief recap of the problem in 30 seconds to one-and-a-half minutes. Focus on two or three main points or recommendations that you want the interviewer to remember. For comparison cases, create a final slide at the beginning by listing products or markets and the criteria for comparison. Fill in this chart as you calculate the numbers, then turn the slide toward the interviewer and walk them through it. This approach is similar to how consultants present their findings to clients.
