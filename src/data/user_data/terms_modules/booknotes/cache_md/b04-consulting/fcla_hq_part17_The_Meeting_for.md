#### Reviewing Meeting for Action
After each meeting for action, review your experience through structured questions. This self-assessment helps you learn and prepare better for future meetings by reflecting on various aspects such as outcomes, resistance handling, and nonverbal cues.
:p How do you review a meeting for action?
??x
Review the meeting using these key questions:
1. What was the outcome?
2. What was the final understanding of choices or possibilities? How did this shift during the meeting?
3. What form did the resistance take?
4. How did you respond to the resistance? Did you take it personally, give more explanation and data, or seek underlying concerns about control and vulnerability?
5. Did you get stuck at any point?
6. What nonverbal messages did you notice?
7. How was the meeting managed compared to how the technical or business problem is being managed?
8. What effect on your relationship with the client did this meeting have?

This reflection helps identify areas for improvement and enhances overall effectiveness in future meetings.

#### Implementation Challenges
In this section, the author highlights the gap between knowing what to do and actually implementing it effectively. It discusses how consultants often focus too much on analysis and recommendations while neglecting the complexity of translating these insights into action. The author emphasizes that implementation is more complex than a rational, step-by-step process; it involves addressing the human aspect and changing mindsets within organizations.
:p What are some key challenges faced by consultants in implementing solutions?
??x
The key challenges include:
1. **Focusing too Much on Analysis**: Consultants often spend excessive time analyzing problems and formulating recommendations but give less attention to translating these insights into practical actions.
2. **Rational vs. Human Processes**: Implementation requires understanding and addressing the human dynamics involved, which is more nuanced than a purely rational approach.
3. **Engineering Mindset Limitations**: Treating change as something that can be installed or engineered like an engineering project fails because change involves living systems, making it more complex.

These challenges highlight the need for a balanced approach that considers both technical and organizational aspects of implementation.
