#### Indirect Expression of Underlying Concerns
When you encounter resistance, it often reflects deeper underlying anxieties that are expressed indirectly. The client feels uncomfortable but does not directly express these concerns, making consultants feel like they're dealing with a victim situation.

If the manager were to be authentic and state their discomfort directly (e.g., "I am concerned I am losing control of this group," or "People expect things from me that we can't deliver"), it would not be considered resistance. In such cases, consultants would provide support rather than engaging in over-explanation or defense.

Resistance occurs when concerns about facing difficult realities and choosing to avoid them are expressed indirectly through various excuses like insufficient data, lack of time, impracticality, budget constraints, or blaming others.

:p What is the reason behind perceiving resistance as a "victim" situation?
??x
When consultants perceive resistance as a "victim" situation, it's because the client’s discomfort and underlying concerns are expressed indirectly. If these were directly stated, such as "I am concerned about losing control," the situation would not be framed as resistance but rather as an opportunity for authentic communication and support.

#### Control in Organizations
Control is highly valued within most organizations. It goes beyond just effectiveness or good performance; maintaining control often takes precedence over achieving better results. Many managers prefer to maintain control even if it means poorer performance.

For instance, a division of a company might keep overly restrictive controls on their management information systems despite proven evidence that these controls hinder productivity improvements. This is because the reward system in organizations is built around gaining more control, responsibility, and authority.

As one ascends in an organization, they realize that proving oneself is part of every role, from entry-level to executive positions. This continuous evaluation through politics can be anxiety-inducing for managers.

#### Identifying Resistance Cues

Background context: In dealing with resistance, it's crucial to understand that client reservations are often expressed indirectly. The goal is to help the line manager express these concerns directly so that they can be effectively addressed. This process involves identifying subtle signs of discomfort or unease in the client.

:p How do you identify the form of resistance taking place?

??x
To identify the form of resistance, focus on nonverbal cues and your own bodily reactions. Pay attention to the client's body language such as movements, posture, and facial expressions. If they are moving away from you, feeling tense or uncomfortable, leaning into another meeting, or making constant gestures like shaking their head or crossing their arms, these could be signs of resistance.

Additionally, listen to yourself: if you start feeling uneasy, bored, or irritated during a conversation, it might indicate that the client is also experiencing some form of resistance. Your body's reactions can serve as early warning signals, helping you pinpoint where the discomfort lies.

:p What are the steps to handle resistance after identifying its form?

??x
After identifying the form of resistance through nonverbal cues and self-awareness, the next step involves clearly stating the nature of this resistance in a neutral manner. This is known as "naming the resistance." For example:

```java
public class ResistanceHandler {
    public void handleResistance() {
        // Example: Identifying that the client is feeling unease about the project
        if (client.isUneasy()) {
            System.out.println("Client seems to be experiencing some level of resistance due to concerns about this project.");
        }
    }
}
```

This code snippet exemplifies how you might translate your observations into a statement. The key here is to avoid interpretations or explanations, as they can alienate the client.

:p How do you pick up cues effectively?

??x
Picking up cues involves closely observing both verbal and nonverbal signals from the client. Here are some specific ways:

- **Nonverbal Observations**: Look for physical signs like constant movement away from the speaker (indicating discomfort), being in a tense posture, making gestures with their fingers or hands, shaking their head frequently while speaking, or bending over toward you as if they were royalty.
  
- **Self-Awareness**: Pay attention to your own body language and emotional reactions. If you feel uncomfortable, bored, or irritated during the conversation, it might indicate that resistance is present.

- **Repetitive Patterns**: Listen for repeated ideas or questions. The client may be reiterating points because expressing them the first time was insufficient.

:p How does trust in your observations help in dealing with resistance?

??x
Trusting your own observations and instincts can help you accurately identify signs of resistance without jumping to conclusions. By focusing on what you see and feel, you avoid the trap of interpreting emotions based on assumptions or personal biases. This approach helps maintain objectivity and ensures that any identified issues are genuinely addressing client concerns.

:p What is the importance of naming the resistance?

??x
Naming the resistance means clearly stating in a neutral manner what form the resistance is taking. Use simple, non-judgmental language to describe the behavior or attitude you've observed. For example:

```java
public class ResistanceHandler {
    public void nameResistance() {
        if (client.isMovingAway()) {
            System.out.println("The client appears to be moving away from us, which could indicate discomfort with the project.");
        }
    }
}
```

This step is crucial because it helps create a shared understanding of the issues without placing blame or causing offense. By naming the resistance, you allow for open discussion and resolution.
