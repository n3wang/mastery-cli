#### Measuring ROI
Background context: Level 5 of measurement, often considered the gold standard, focuses on determining the Return On Investment (ROI) for a project. This is relevant when an initiative closely aligns with strategic goals, involves significant investment, has a large target group, and garners interest from senior executives.

An important step in measuring ROI is to calculate both the monetary value of benefits and the monetary costs of the initiative. The standard formula for determining ROI is:
$$
\text{ROI} = \left( \frac{\text{Net Benefits}}{\text{Costs}} \right) \times 100\%
$$

Where Net Benefits = Total Benefits - Costs.

Example: If an initiative costs $50,000 and generates benefits of $75,000, the ROI would be:
$$
\text{ROI} = \left( \frac{75,000 - 50,000}{50,000} \right) \times 100\% = 50\%
$$

The goal is to obtain credible data for both benefits and costs.
:p What are the key steps in measuring ROI according to the text?
??x
Key steps in measuring ROI include:
1. Agreeing with clients if an ROI measurement is important.
2. Setting a realistic and achievable ROI objective (e.g., 25%).
3. Using performance objectives linked to initiative performance.
4. Utilizing business results metrics already obtained and reported by the organization.
5. Valuing measures that are relevant to your clients.

For example, if an initiative costs $50,000 and generates benefits of $75,000:
$$
\text{ROI} = \left( \frac{75,000 - 50,000}{50,000} \right) \times 100\% = 50\%
$$

Ensure the data is credible by following guidelines such as using only the first year of benefits in short-term solutions and not including unsupported extreme data items.
