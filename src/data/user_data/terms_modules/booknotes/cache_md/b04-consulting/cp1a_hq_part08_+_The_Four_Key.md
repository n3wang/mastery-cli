#### Profit and Loss Scenario
Background context: The profit-and-loss (P&L) scenario is a fundamental type of case interview question. It involves analyzing why profits might be down despite an increase in sales. To solve this, you need to decompose the P&L equation into its components.

The key formula for P&L analysis is:
$$\text{Profits} = \text{(Revenue - Costs)} = (Q \times P) - [(Q \times VC) + FC]$$
Where \( Q \) represents quantity, \( P \) price, \( VC \) variable costs, and \( FC \) fixed costs.

When faced with a question like: "Our client manufactures high-end athletic footwear. Sales are up but profits are down in the U.S. What’s going on, and how do we fix it?" you should immediately think about the P&L structure as outlined above. Additionally, consider the environment \( E \) (external factors) and market \( M \) (industry trends).

:p How would you approach a profit-and-loss scenario interview question?
??x
You would start by understanding that profits are calculated as:
$$\text{Profits} = \text{(Revenue - Costs)}$$
To dissect this further, consider the revenue equation:
$$R = Q \times P$$
And the costs equation:
$$C = (Q \times VC) + FC$$

You should ask clarifying questions such as:

1. "Have our competitors' profits also fallen?" This helps determine if it's an industry-wide issue or a company-specific problem.
2. "By how much have profits fallen?" to quantify the extent of the issue.

If it is an industry-wide problem, you would then look at external factors \( E \) and market dynamics \( M \). For instance:
- If it’s a retailer, consider consumer confidence, disposable income, unemployment rate, and petrol prices.
- If it’s a manufacturer, examine how the dollar is doing against other currencies, interest rates, petrol prices, and commodities.

If it's just your company facing the issue, you would investigate internal factors such as increased costs, lower revenues, or outdated products. The objective is to structure the problem by first considering external factors before diving into specific issues.

Background context: Environmental events like hurricanes, tornadoes, mudslides, and wildfires can affect companies' profits. For instance, some companies may see their profits drop due to damage, while others might benefit from increased demand for emergency services or reconstruction materials.

#### Market Entry Feasibility
Background context on assessing whether entering a new market makes good business sense. This includes competitor analysis, product pricing, and barriers to entry.
:p What are some key factors to consider when investigating the market for entry feasibility?
??x
- Who are the competitors, and what size market share do they have?
- How do their products differ from ours?
- How will we price our products or services?
- Are substitutions available?
- Are there any barriers to entry? Examples might include lack of brand or street cred, capital requirements, access to raw materials, access to distribution channels, lack of human capital, and government policy. Also, industries dominated by a small number of big players can be a barrier.
- Are there barriers to an exit? How would we exit if this market sours?
- What are the risks? (For example, changing market regulations or technology.)
