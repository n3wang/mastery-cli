#### Action vs. Research Approach in Discovery
The text outlines two approaches to the discovery phase: a research approach focused on understanding, and an action approach aimed at practical implementation. The emphasis is on action rather than mere understanding.

:p What are the key differences between the research and action approaches during the discovery phase?
??x
In the research approach:
- Focuses on all factors impacting the situation.
- Is comprehensive and complete in the initial stages.
- Involves extensive data collection and analysis.
- Aims for objectivity, eliminating bias.

In contrast, the action approach:
- Focuses only on factors under the client's control.
- Simplifies inquiry to focus on next steps.
- Uses everyday language and avoids overwhelming details.
- Emphasizes client involvement at each stage.
- Values feelings and perceptions alongside data.
- Prioritizes client acceptance of outcomes.

The research approach is more thorough but can be overwhelming, while the action approach is more streamlined and practical. Both approaches have their merits, but the emphasis on action in this context highlights the need for consultants to balance thorough understanding with practical implementation.

#### Redefining the Problem Statement
In many consulting projects, managers come to consultants with a defined problem or presenting problem. However, this initial description of the issue (presenting problem) is often incomplete and can lead to ineffective solutions if not properly redefined. The role of the consultant is to delve deeper into the situation, uncover the underlying causes, and provide a clear and simple picture of the true nature of the problem.

Managers may start with a surface-level explanation for issues such as:
- People are restless.
- Equipment isn't working.
- Output is down.
- Processes like invoicing are flawed.
- Projects are running late.

These initial explanations (presenting problems) can be inaccurate, leading to ineffective attempts at solving the actual issue. The goal of the consultant is to redefine these presenting problems by uncovering deeper issues related to:
- Technical or business problem
- Management practices and politics
- Attitudes of people involved

This redefinition allows for more effective recommendations on how to proceed.
:p What is the role of a consultant in relation to the initial problem statement provided by the client?
??x
The role of a consultant is to redefine the presenting problem, which often provides an incomplete or inaccurate understanding of the issue. The consultant's objective is to uncover and clearly define the underlying causes of the problems, leading to more effective solutions. This involves:
- Assessing both technical/business issues and management practices.
- Understanding the attitudes and behaviors of people involved.
- Identifying gaps in communication, support, and structure.

By redefining the problem, consultants can help clients address root causes rather than just symptoms, thereby making more informed and actionable recommendations.

#### Example of Redefining a Problem Statement
Consider an example where a large technical organization was experiencing high turnover rates among new employees. Initially, managers blamed low salaries, housing scarcity, and unrealistic expectations as the main reasons for employee departures.

However, after further investigation by internal consultants, different factors were identified:
1. New hires received orientation but did not get meaningful assignments until almost a year into their tenure.
2. They lacked regular feedback from supervisors, making it difficult to understand their role or career prospects.
3. First-line managers were under immense pressure and had limited time for mentoring new employees.

These insights led to a redefinition of the problem: inadequate support, attention, and meaningful tasks were causing turnover. This shift in perspective allowed management to develop more effective strategies to retain talent.
:p How did the consultants redefine the initial problem statement in this example?
??x
The consultants redefined the initial problem statement by identifying that the main issue was not low salaries or housing scarcity but rather inadequate support, attention, and meaningful tasks for new hires. Specifically:
- New hires were not given responsible assignments until almost a year into their tenure.
- They lacked regular feedback from supervisors, making it difficult to understand their role or career prospects.
- First-line managers were under immense pressure and had limited time for mentoring.

This redefinition led to more effective strategies such as contract agreements between supervisors and new hires, better task assignment, and increased time devoted by managers to support new employees. This shift in perspective from external factors (salary, housing) to internal management practices allowed the organization to address the root cause of employee turnover.

In action-oriented discovery, consultants often focus on solving technical or business problems. However, understanding how these problems are managed by the client is crucial for effective solutions. Management style, politics, and organizational dynamics significantly impact problem resolution. Consultants typically discuss management issues informally during breaks or social interactions with peers. Consultants must decide whether to address these social system problems directly or indirectly. Dealing with them directly is riskier but necessary for a holistic approach to problem-solving. #### Social System Problems in Consulting

**Background Context and Explanation:**
Social system problems refer to the interpersonal dynamics and organizational structures that influence how technical or business issues are managed. These include factors such as team culture, communication patterns, and leadership styles.

While consultants primarily focus on solving technical or business problems, recognizing social system problems is crucial for effective solutions. Ignoring these issues can result in solutions that are technically sound but fail to address underlying organizational challenges.

These social systems often come up informally during breaks, social interactions, or casual discussions among colleagues. While they might not be explicitly part of the client's request, addressing them indirectly through observations and subtle suggestions is a practical approach.

However, consultants must navigate these conversations carefully to avoid appearing overly critical or judgmental. Balancing professional advice with respect for organizational norms requires tact and discretion.
