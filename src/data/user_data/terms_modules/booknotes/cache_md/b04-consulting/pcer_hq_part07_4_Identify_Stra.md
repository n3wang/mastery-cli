#### Performance Problems and Multiple Causes
Performance problems often have multiple causes. Single solutions rarely address the underlying issues effectively, leading to temporary fixes or no improvement at all.

:p Why do single solutions often fail to solve performance problems?
??x
Single solutions frequently fail to solve complex performance problems for several reasons:
1. **Over-Simplification:** Performance issues are often multifaceted and require a holistic approach.
2. **Root Cause Ignored:** Focusing only on the symptoms can lead to overlooking critical underlying causes.
3. **Lack of Sustainability:** Temporary fixes may work in the short term but do not address long-term issues.

#### Re-framing Requests as Performance Consultants
When clients present solutions, it is an opportunity to reframe their requests and focus on the underlying business performance goals. This approach transforms your role from a tactical provider of solutions to a strategic partner.

:p How can you re-frame client requests to align with broader business objectives?
??x
Re-framing client requests involves asking powerful questions that shift the focus from specific solutions to the desired business outcomes. Here’s how:

1. **Understand the Client's Goals:** Ask about their overarching goals and what success looks like.
2. **Identify Performance Gaps:** Determine where current performance falls short of these goals.
3. **Explore Root Causes:** Delve into why there are gaps in performance.

Example questions to reframe a client request:
- "What specific outcomes do you hope to achieve with this solution?"
- "How does addressing this issue contribute to your business objectives?"
- "Can you describe the situation leading up to this problem?"

By asking such questions, you can help clients articulate their broader goals and identify multiple solutions that address underlying issues.

Example of re-framing a client request:
```python
def reframe_request(request):
    """
    Function to reframe a client's request into a strategic performance objective.
    
    :param request: The original solution-focused request
    
    This function will help transform the request into a broader business goal.
    """
    print(f"Let's start by understanding your goals: {request}")
    
    # Step 1: Clarify Business Objectives
    step_1 = "Can you tell me more about your overarching business objectives?"
    
    # Step 2: Identify Performance Gaps
    step_2 = "Where do you see the gaps in current performance that this solution aims to address?"
    
    # Step 3: Explore Root Causes
    step_3 = "What are the underlying factors contributing to these gaps?"
    
    print(step_1)
    print(step_2)
    print(step_3)
```

#### Reframing in Context
Reframing is often used when clients request solutions that address surface-level issues without considering broader organizational needs. It involves moving from a tactical focus (specific solution) to a strategic one (underlying performance or business goals).
:p How can reframing help uncover unmet business needs?
??x
By focusing on the desired outcomes and their connections to broader business objectives, consultants can identify gaps in the client's understanding or plans. This process helps ensure that any proposed solutions are aligned with overall business strategies.

For instance, if a client asks for team-building activities because they experience conflict, reframing might reveal that the real need is to improve communication skills across departments, which could be more effectively addressed through training programs.

#### Using Powerful Questions in Reframing
Powerful questions guide clients toward identifying their true needs and goals. These are different from solution-oriented questions and help uncover underlying issues or missed opportunities.
:p What types of questions should a performance consultant ask during the reframing process?
??x
Performance consultants should ask SHOULD questions to identify desired business and performance outcomes, and IS questions to understand current states relative to those SHOULDs.

Example: "What specific outcomes do you want to achieve by improving your team's performance?" (SHOULD question) vs. "How would you describe the current state of your teams' interactions?" (IS question).

This approach helps clients articulate their goals more clearly and consider various aspects of their needs.

#### Gaps Logic in Reframing
The Gaps logic helps identify discrepancies between current states and desired outcomes, guiding consultants to uncover necessary information or improvements. It involves moving from individual capabilities to business results.
:p How does the Gaps logic support the reframing process?
??x
The Gaps logic identifies areas where there are discrepancies between what is currently happening (IS) and what should be happening (SHOULD). By using this framework, consultants can help clients understand that their requests might overlook important information or broader impacts.

For example, if a client wants to enhance team-building activities, reframing through the Gaps logic could reveal gaps in communication skills, organizational alignment, or leadership effectiveness.

CAUSE questions are designed to uncover the underlying reasons for gaps in business results or performance by exploring potential root causes from various perspectives: external to the organization, within the organization, and within individuals. The example shows how a manager's request was not connected to the performance consultant's response, while a more appropriate response would start from the client's observed issues.
:p Why is it important to use compelling logic in questioning?

For example:

#### Question on Gaps Map
Background context explaining what a Gaps Map is. It helps in identifying the difference between current and desired performance, ensuring that questions are focused on specific elements of this gap.
:p What is a Gaps Map?
??x
A Gaps Map is a tool used to identify the difference between current and desired performance. By focusing on specific elements within the map, you can ensure that your questions are targeted and relevant, leading to more actionable insights.

For example, in a scenario with new employees:
- Current Performance (IS): What issues have been observed?
- Desired Performance (SHOULD): What improvements do you want to see?

This helps in formulating focused questions like: "What actions do technicians typically take when the process is outside specifications?"

#### Business Goal Discussion and Transition to Performance Side
Background context: The discussion transitions from business goals and gaps to performance by focusing on the operational details of how these goals are being tracked. This involves identifying specific metrics and current results.

:p If a client mentions experiencing an increase in preventable accidents, how do you start the conversation?
??x
You begin by understanding the client's primary safety goal for their manufacturing organization:
- "What are the safety goals for the manufacturing organization?"
- "How are we currently tracking against each of these goals?"

Once gaps between desired and current results are identified (business gap), ask causal questions to understand why performance is lacking:
- "Tell me more about the reasons that safety results are declining."

Transitioning from here, move back to the performance side by focusing on what employees need to do differently or better if the goals are to be achieved:
- "Clearly the operators are key to improving safety results. What is it they need to do more, better, or differently if the safety goals are to be achieved?"

For example, you might ask:
```python
def assess_safety(current_accidents, target_accidents):
    # Calculate difference between current and target accidents
    gap = target_accidents - current_accidents
    
    if gap > 0:
        print("There's a gap of", gap, "accidents.")
    else:
        print("Safety performance is on track or better.")

# Example usage
assess_safety(15, 10)
```

#### Business Need and Gap Identification
Background context: When the client presents a business need or gap directly, start by focusing on the business side of the Gaps Map to gather operational information before moving to performance issues.

:p How do you handle a situation where the client has already identified a business goal or gap?
??x
When the client identifies a specific business issue (e.g., increased accidents), begin with questions that uncover the underlying goals and current status:
- "What are the safety goals for the manufacturing organization?"
- "How are we currently tracking against each of these goals?"

Once gaps between desired and actual results are identified, ask causal questions to understand root causes:
- "Tell me more about the reasons that safety results are declining."

Then transition back to performance by focusing on what employees need to improve:
- "Clearly the operators are key to improving safety results. What is it they need to do more, better, or differently if the safety goals are to be achieved?"

For example:
```python
def track_safety_goals(current_accidents, target_accidents):
    # Track current vs. targeted accidents
    gap = target_accidents - current_accidents
    
    if gap > 0:
        print("Current status:", current_accidents)
        print("Target:", target_accidents)
        print("Gap to close:", gap)
    else:
        print("Safety performance is on track or better.")

# Example usage
track_safety_goals(15, 10)
```
