#### Essential Wants from the Client
Background context: Identifying what you want from the client is critical during a contracting meeting to ensure that all parties are aligned on project goals and expectations.

:p What are your essential wants from the client?

??x
Essential wants typically include non-negotiable aspects of the project. For example, in a software development contract:
- **Essential Want**: The client agrees to provide timely and complete requirements.
- **Non-Essential Want**: The client provides feedback on prototypes within one week after each release.

Failing to secure these essential wants can jeopardize the success of the project. Make sure they are clearly defined and prioritized during the contracting meeting.

#### Desirable Wants from the Client
Background context: While essential wants must be secured, desirable wants enhance the client's experience but may not be critical for project completion.

:p What are your desirable wants from the client?

??x
Desirable wants include elements that would improve the outcome of the project without necessarily being deal-breakers. For example:
- **Desirable Want**: The client provides regular progress reports.
- **Non-Essential Want**: The client participates in weekly review meetings.

These can be discussed and negotiated, but if they are not achieved, it should not significantly impact the overall success of the project.

For instance, in a marketing strategy consultation, you might desire:
- **Desirable Want**: The client offers access to key decision-makers.
- **Non-Essential Want**: The client shares preliminary market research data.

Ensure these wants are communicated clearly and understood by the client.

#### Offering Value to the Client
Background context: Understanding what you offer to the client in terms of technical, personal, and facilitative support is essential for a successful contracting meeting. This helps in aligning your services with their needs and expectations.

:p What do you offer the client?

??x
You should be clear about the value you bring to the project. For example:
- **Technically**: Expertise in specific technologies or methodologies.
- **Personally**: Availability, experience, and communication style.
- **Facilitation**: Assistance in improving how they work together, such as streamlining processes or enhancing team collaboration.

For instance, if you are consulting a business on their IT infrastructure:
- **Technically Offered**: Knowledge of the latest cybersecurity practices.
- **Personality Offered**: A collaborative approach and regular communication updates.
- **Facilitative Offer**: Helping to train staff on new systems and procedures.

Ensure your offerings are aligned with what the client values most in a consulting project.

#### Checklist for Planning a Contracting Meeting
Background context: The checklist helps ensure that all necessary steps are covered before conducting a contracting meeting. It ensures that both parties are prepared, focused, and aware of their roles and responsibilities.

:p What questions should you answer when planning a contracting meeting?

??x
1. **Expected Imbalance in Responsibility**: Will the client treat you as an expert or as a hands-on collaborator?
2. **Client Wants**: List essential and desirable wants.
3. **Offered Value**: Define your technical, personal, and facilitative contributions.

These questions help set the stage for a productive meeting by ensuring all key points are covered and both parties are aligned on expectations.

For example:
- Expected Imbalance: The client will treat you as an expert in IT security.
- Essential Wants: Timely requirement provision.
- Desirable Wants: Regular progress reports.

#### Reviewing the Contracting Meeting
Background context: Evaluating the contracting meeting after it has taken place is crucial for understanding its effectiveness and identifying areas for improvement. This helps in preparing better for future meetings.

:p What questions should you ask to review a contracting meeting?

??x
1. **Client-Consultant Balance**: How did the participation balance between client and consultant?
2. **Possible Client Wants**: List all possible wants, both technical and personal.
3. **Key Decision Makers**: Are key decision-makers present? Who can make decisions?
4. **Anticipated Resistance**: What resistance or reservations might arise?

These questions help in assessing the effectiveness of the meeting and preparing for potential challenges.

For example:
- Client-Consultant Balance: The meeting was mostly 50/50, but the client initiated more discussions.
- Possible Wants: Better project documentation; improved communication channels.
- Key Decision Makers: The CEO is not present; key stakeholders are involved.

#### External Consultant's Advantage
Background context: External consultants operate outside the organization they are consulting for, which offers them a broader client base and less risk of being seen as biased by internal stakeholders. They can bounce between different levels without the same level of scrutiny or expectations.
:p How does an external consultant’s position outside the organization benefit their work?
??x
External consultants have several advantages due to their position outside the organization they are consulting for. Primarily, this allows them a wider market for their services as clients trust their objectivity more readily. They face fewer biases and can navigate different organizational levels with greater flexibility.

Moreover, external consultants are not bound by the same internal politics and pressures that internal consultants experience. This independence can lead to more innovative solutions and better client relationships without the risk of being seen as captured by the organization’s internal forces.

Background context: Internal consultants often have a dual role—they must operate under mandates set by their superiors while also making choices based on what is best for the organization. This high-risk proposition requires them to make explicit the limitations and costs of mandated changes, even if they lack final decision-making authority.
:p How does operating with both mandate and choice create challenges for internal consultants?
??x
Operating as an internal consultant involves a dual role where you must balance mandates from superiors with your own professional judgment. This creates high-risk scenarios because while you may have to follow certain directives, you also need to advocate effectively for what is best for the organization.

This dual role requires making explicit the limitations and costs of mandated changes, as even without final decision-making authority, questioning processes remains crucial. The challenge lies in navigating this fine line between compliance and innovation, ensuring that both mandates and choices contribute positively to organizational goals.

#### Internal Consultant's Dilemma Context
Internal consultants face a complex set of expectations and norms that can create significant challenges. These norms are often implicit and can be contradictory, making it difficult to navigate between fulfilling them while also providing effective consulting services.

Cultural norms include:
- "No matter what, get the job done."
- "If the technical issue is that important, don't worry about the sensitivities of the client. Bedside manner is overrated."
- "Don’t upset or antagonize the client."

The key dilemma for internal consultants lies in balancing these expectations with their actual capabilities.

:p What are some examples of the cultural norms faced by internal consultants?
??x
Examples include:
1. "No matter what, get the job done." - This expectation can pressure consultants to push through difficult situations without considering the client's sensitivities.
2. "If the technical issue is that important, don't worry about the sensitivities of the client. Bedside manner is overrated." - Consultants may feel they need to focus solely on solving problems rather than maintaining good client relations.
3. "Don’t upset or antagonize the client." - This norm can create a challenge when consultants must address difficult issues that might cause conflict.

These norms reflect broader organizational values and expectations, but often conflict with practical realities.

#### Boss's Expectations
The boss can set unrealistic expectations on internal consultants, which may be unfulfillable. These expectations can lead to internal conflicts between consultants and their clients if not properly managed. Common expectations include:
- "Convince the client to do what you recommend."
- "Sell the company way."
- "Every problem can be solved."

These pressures create a dilemma for consultants as they try to balance these demands with the need to provide effective consulting services.

:p What are some examples of the boss's unrealistic expectations?
??x
Examples include:
1. "Convince the client to do what you recommend." - This expectation may pressure consultants into pushing recommendations that might not align with the client’s needs or wishes.
2. "Sell the company way." - Consultants must ensure their advice reflects positively on the organization, which can be challenging if there are conflicts of interest.
3. "Every problem can be solved." - This implies a guarantee of success in all projects, which is unrealistic and can lead to overcommitment.

These expectations reflect broader organizational goals but often put consultants under immense pressure.
