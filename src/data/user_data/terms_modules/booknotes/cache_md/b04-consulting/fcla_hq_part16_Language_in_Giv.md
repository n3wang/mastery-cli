#### Descriptive vs Judgmental Language

When providing feedback, it's essential to use descriptive language rather than judgmental terms. Descriptive statements focus on observable facts and behaviors within an organization, while judgmental statements impose personal opinions or evaluations.

Avoid using evaluative words like "weak," "strong," "incompetent," "indecisive," "dictatorial," and "controlling." Instead, provide specific details about the situation and avoid vague stereotypes. For example:

- **Descriptive:** "The boss frequently dominates meetings by interrupting others."
- **Judgmental:** "The boss is dictatorial."

:p How can consultants improve their feedback by avoiding judgmental language?
??x
Consultants should focus on providing specific, observable behaviors rather than making personal judgments or evaluations. For instance, instead of saying "The team lacks leadership," a better approach would be to say "During meetings, the team frequently defers decisions to one individual." This way, the feedback is descriptive and actionable.

#### Specific vs Global Statements

Global statements are too broad and vague to be useful in pinpointing specific problems. For instance, saying "We have a problem with decision-making" doesn't help identify where or how exactly this issue manifests.

Instead, make your statements as specific as possible. Focus on the actions or behaviors that lead to the problem. 

:p How can consultants make their feedback more actionable by using specific statements?
??x
Consultants should provide concrete examples and details about what is happening within the organization rather than making general claims. For example:

- **Global:** "Our decision-making process is flawed."
- **Specific:** "During team meetings, decisions are often delayed because members do not share their thoughts in a timely manner."

This approach helps stakeholders understand exactly where the issue lies and can lead to more effective solutions.

#### Brief vs Lengthy Explanations

Avoid long explanations when providing feedback. Most questions about methods or recommendations can be answered succinctly, typically in one paragraph.

If you find yourself explaining at length, try to distill your points into a concise statement. Recognize that resistance might be coming from the client and address it directly by acknowledging their reaction without re-explaining your point.

:p How should consultants handle lengthy explanations when giving feedback?
??x
Consultants should keep their explanations brief and focused on providing clear, actionable information. If they find themselves going into detail or repeating points, they should acknowledge that resistance is likely present and address it directly by saying, "It seems like you are concerned about this point. Let's discuss how we can move forward."

By acknowledging the client's resistance, consultants can help build trust and foster a more productive conversation.

#### Presenting the Picture vs Courtroom Drama

In consulting projects, presenting the picture as courtroom drama—where the consultant is either judge, jury, or prosecutor—can be counterproductive. Instead, view yourself as an impartial witness who provides clear and specific observations.

Consultants should avoid taking on roles that make them appear authoritative or judgmental. This can lead to a lack of trust and resistance from clients.

:p How should consultants present their feedback in a way that avoids courtroom drama?
??x
Consultants should focus on being impartial witnesses, presenting accurate information without making judgments or taking sides. They should provide clear, specific observations and allow the client to draw conclusions themselves.

For example:

- **Courtroom Drama:** "You are clearly wrong about your decision-making process."
- **Witness Role:** "I observed that during meetings, one person often dominates the conversation, which can lead to missed ideas."

By sticking to a witness role, consultants maintain neutrality and encourage open dialogue.
