#### Comparison Groups

When it's not possible to use a pilot group, identify a similar group for future involvement and use them as the comparison group. This naturally occurring control group allows for comparing performance between two groups without purposefully withholding an opportunity.

:p When is it appropriate to use a comparison group instead of a control/experimental design?

#### Trend-Line Analysis

Trend-line analysis tracks actual performance over time, projects what future performance might look like without intervention, then compares this projection with post-intervention data. The difference between projected and actual outcomes provides an estimate of consulting impact.

Background context: It is simpler and less resource-intensive than a control group approach. Useful when historical data are available.
:p How does trend-line analysis work?
??x
Trend-line analysis works by tracking the actual performance over time, projecting future trends based on existing data, and then comparing these projections with post-intervention outcomes to estimate the impact of changes.

For example, if error rates were increasing at a rate of 5 errors per month (60 in total for six months), a trend line might predict an increase without intervention. After implementing solutions, actual performance is compared against this projection.

#### Step 3: Calculate the Change in Performance Data
After determining the unit of measure and its monetary value, the third step is to calculate the change in performance data resulting from the initiative. This involves isolating the effects of the initiative on the business measures.

For example, if a project completion time was reduced by 10%, this would be represented as ΔP = -10%.

#### Step 5: Calculate the Annual Value of the Improvement
In this final step, you calculate the total value of the improvement by multiplying the annual performance change (ΔP) by the unit value (V) of the measure.

For example, if reducing one customer complaint results in a $50 gain and this is done 100 times annually, then the annual value would be \( A\Delta P \times V = 100 \times \$50 = \$5000 \).

:p What is the fifth step in calculating the ROI?
??x
The fifth step involves calculating the total value of the improvement by multiplying the annual performance change (ΔP) by the unit value (V). This gives you the monetary benefit of the initiative.

#### Input from Internal and External Experts

When historical data is not available, experts can provide estimates of the cost or value of a unit of improvement. This method involves consulting individuals with relevant knowledge and respect within management.

For instance, in a project to improve grievance resolution, internal labor relations staff might estimate that each average grievance costs $6,500 when considering time and direct costs.

:p How can you use expert input to determine the monetary value?

To use expert input for determining monetary values:

1. Identify experts with relevant knowledge.
2. Explain the full scope of what is needed.
3. Provide as many specifics as possible.
4. Ask them to provide estimates on a unit of improvement.

For example, in grievance resolution, you might ask internal labor relations staff or external consultants how much time and direct costs are associated with resolving an average grievance.
