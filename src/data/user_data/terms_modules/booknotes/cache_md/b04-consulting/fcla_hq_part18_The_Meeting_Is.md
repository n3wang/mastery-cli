#### Redefining the Role of Participants
The text suggests that a shift in mindset is necessary for effective meetings. One example given is when entertainers announce upfront that they are "the Southern County Rounders" and that attendees are part of the audience. This declaration emphasizes that participants have an active role to play in creating the experience.

The author proposes asking questions like: 
- How valuable an experience do you plan to have in this session or effort?
- How engaged and active do you plan to be?
- How much risk are you willing to take?

Participants should rate these questions on a five-point scale from 1 (low) to 5 (high). The text then suggests breaking into small groups of three for discussion.

:p What questions does the author suggest asking participants before a meeting or effort begins?
??x
The author suggests asking:
- How valuable an experience do you plan to have in this session or effort— not what kind of experience you want, but what kind of value do you plan to have?
- How engaged and active do you plan to be?
- How much risk are you willing to take?
- How invested are you in the quality of the experience of those around you? What is your level of concern about the well-being of the larger group?

Participants should rate these questions on a five-point scale: 1 (low), 5 (high) and then share their answers with each other in small groups.

#### Balancing Presentation and Participation
The text emphasizes that the balance between presentation and participation is often overly skewed towards presentation. Most meetings focus heavily on presentations while treating participation as an afterthought.

Question-and-answer sessions are not considered true engagement; they are at best clarifying, and too often, they are used for persuasion rather than active collaboration. The passive contract between leader and participant needs to be renegotiated early in the process to ensure better alignment with modern meeting purposes.

:p Why is the balance between presentation and participation important according to the text?
??x
The balance between presentation and participation is crucial because traditional meetings often focus too much on presentations by managers or consultants, neglecting active involvement from participants. This imbalance means that participants are passive consumers rather than active contributors. To improve engagement and effectiveness, the role of participants should be highlighted early in the meeting process.
