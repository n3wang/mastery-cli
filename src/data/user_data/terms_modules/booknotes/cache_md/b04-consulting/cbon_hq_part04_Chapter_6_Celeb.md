#### Thought Leadership and Expertise
Thought leadership is a concept that denotes expertise and status as a conveyor of intellectual capital. The term "thought leadership" has entered common usage, but it can be expressed differently over time. Some prefer "results leadership." Regardless, being a thought leader means creating true knowledge by implementing tried methods and then sharing those insights.

The National Speakers Association's study found that buyers primarily sought expertise when hiring speakers. Today, notable thought leaders like Marshall Goldsmith in coaching, Seth Godin in creativity, Jonah Berger in influence, Marty Seligman in positive psychology, and the author themselves in solo consulting share common characteristics:

- They create intellectual capital which translates into valuable property.
- Their approaches are public and not afraid of being emulated or stolen since their brands are strong enough to distinguish their work.
- They become cited and quoted as authorities on topics. 
- They are unafraid of failing, recognizing that failure is a part of innovation.

The objective here is to create your own celebrity by self-authoring your path in the market.

#### Failure Tolerance
Thought leaders embrace failure as part of innovation. They introduce new ideas and methods into their niches without being shocked when some fail. The key message is that not failing means not trying hard enough.

If someone consistently fails more than they succeed, their thought leadership might be questioned or revoked.
:p How do thought leaders handle failure?
??x
Thought leaders view failure as an essential part of the learning and innovation process. They introduce new ideas and methods into their fields without fear, knowing that some will fail. This mindset encourages experimentation and growth rather than discouragement. Success is seen as a combination of both success and failure.
