#### Verify the Objective(s)
Background context: In any consulting case scenario, it is crucial to confirm the objectives with the interviewer. This ensures that you focus on the right goals and avoids answering a different question.

Relevant formulas or data: None specific, but understanding the client's expectations and success criteria are important in setting your approach.

:p How should you verify the objectives of the case?
??x
You should explicitly ask if there are any other objectives or goals beyond the initial ones provided. This is done by saying something like, "One objective is to raise profits. Are there any other objectives or goals that I should know about?" If no additional objectives are mentioned, you can proceed focusing on the main goal.

For example, if the interviewer says, "Yes, we also want to improve customer satisfaction," this information becomes part of your overall case structure.

#### Ask Clarifying Questions
Background context: Consulting interviews often involve complex scenarios with details that may not be immediately clear. Asking clarifying questions helps ensure you have all necessary information to proceed effectively.

Relevant formulas or data: None specific, but asking for clarification is key in gathering the right data points.

:p What kind of clarifying questions should you ask?
??x
Clarifying questions are essential when you don’t fully understand a situation or need additional information. For example, if dealing with a new market entry case, you might ask, "Why does the company want to enter this market?" Or for a revenue growth problem, you could ask, "What have revenues done over the last three years?"

For instance, if entering a new market, you should inquire about the reason for expansion.

#### Label the Case and Lay Out Your Structure
Background context: Structuring your approach to solving a case is critical. This step involves deciding on an initial structure and presenting it clearly to the interviewer.

Relevant formulas or data: None specific, but MECE (Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive) structures are often used in consulting.

:p How do you label the case and lay out your structure?
??x
Labeling the case and laying out your structure involves deciding which aspects of the problem to analyze and in what order. You should think about which sections of the scenario are most relevant and organize them logically. For example, if entering a new market, you might break down your analysis into three buckets: analyzing the client, analyzing the new market, and examining different ways to enter the market.

Presenting this structure to the interviewer involves walking through it step-by-step. Start by mentioning each bucket first (e.g., "First, I will analyze the client..."), then delve into the specific points under each heading.

For example:
- Client: Current business model, strategic goals
- Market: Demographics, competition, trends
- Entry Strategy: Direct entry vs. partnership, etc.

#### State Your Hypothesis
Background context: Stating a hypothesis early in the case helps narrow down the scope and provides a clear direction for your analysis.

Relevant formulas or data: None specific, but having an initial theory can guide your problem-solving process.

:p How should you state your hypothesis?
??x
Consulting firms often value stating a hypothesis early on. Even if it turns out to be incorrect later in the case, it helps narrow down potential solutions and keeps the analysis linear. For example, in a profit and loss scenario, you might hypothesize that "Profits have fallen because costs have risen."

Present your initial thoughts as a hypothesis and be prepared to update it as new information comes in.

For instance:
- Hypothesis: Profits have declined due to increased operational costs.

#### Cost/Margins
Background context: Understanding costs and margins is crucial for assessing a company's financial health and potential profitability. High costs can erode profit margins, impacting overall business performance.

For example, if production costs increase while selling prices remain constant, profit margins will decrease.
:p What should you examine regarding the cost structure of a company?
??x
Examine fixed and variable costs associated with each product or service. Calculate gross margins (revenue - cost) to understand profitability per unit sold.

For instance, if raw material costs for Product X increase by 20%, this will impact its overall margin unless selling prices are adjusted.
