#### Structures of Engagement

Background context: The text highlights the importance of creating structures for engagement during implementation phases of projects or meetings. These structures aim to promote accountability, responsibility, and connection rather than just following a rigid plan.

:p What are some key aspects of implementing engagement in a meeting?

??x
Key aspects include:
- Tilting the balance towards participation over presentation.
- Providing space for people to voice their concerns and opinions openly.
- Helping define choices for clients at multiple levels of the organization.
- Structuring time for serious dialogue on how changes will occur.
- Bringing the power of place into each event.

For example, a facilitator might say:
"Let's ensure that every participant has an opportunity to share their thoughts. We can structure our next session by breaking it into segments where we first discuss potential solutions and then prioritize them."

This approach helps in building internal commitment through meaningful interaction and dialogue.

??x

#### Not Knowing as a Leadership Stance
The text suggests that "not knowing" should be embraced by leaders as it encourages exploration and discovery. This stance contrasts with high-control, predetermined thinking which often hampers genuine learning.

:p How does the text suggest leadership should address the issue of packaged answers?
??x
According to the text, packaged answers and solutions are problematic because they reinforce high-control, predetermined thinking, which is counterproductive when addressing clients' issues. Instead, leaders need to embrace:

1. **Surprise and Discovery**: Introduce elements that surprise participants and encourage them to discover new ideas.
2. **Ambiguity and Questions**: Pose ambiguous questions rather than providing clear answers.
3. **Risk and Tension**: Create a space where risks are acceptable and tensions can arise, leading to deeper discussions.

By shifting from packaged solutions to more open-ended methods, leaders can foster an environment of genuine learning and exploration, thereby addressing the client's problems effectively.
